Buying Used Motorcycle Gloves — What to Know

Are used motorcycle gloves safe to buy?

Yes. Gloves don't have a single-impact replacement rule like helmets. Inspect the palm padding and scaphoid protection (if present), check knuckle armor for cracks, examine stitching at the seams and fingertips, and make sure the wrist closure still provides a snug fit.

Summer vs. winter gloves — what's the difference?

Summer gloves use perforated leather or mesh for airflow and are typically shorter-cuffed. Winter gloves are insulated (Thinsulate or Primaloft) with a waterproof liner. All-season gloves layer a removable liner inside a waterproof outer shell. Buy for your primary riding season.

Gauntlet vs. short-cuff gloves — which should I choose?

Gauntlet gloves (long cuff, worn over jacket sleeves) offer more wrist and forearm protection and seal out wind — ideal for highway and track riding. Short-cuff gloves slip under sleeves and are easier to put on for commuting. If protection is the priority, go gauntlet.

How much should I pay for used motorcycle gloves?

Quality gloves that retail for $80–$150 typically sell used for $30–$80 in good condition. High-end race gloves ($200+) can be found used for $60–$100. Gloves wear faster than jackets due to constant grip — inspect condition carefully before buying.
